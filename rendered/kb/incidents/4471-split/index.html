The #4471 page says the ticket is one issue with one fix queued. Its own table says otherwise: it renders two causes, and a note that some reports are a one-day move. The subject-line rule (decision 0001) merged them, because every one of these reports says “wrong date” in some words. The fix queued for the parser does not fix the other two.

#4471 holds three problems. Each now has its own ticket, below. The counts use the desk's own view (`causes.ts`) of the export, and come from it at render time.

Outcome

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October:

  • parser: fixed by fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket #1 closed as completed.
  • hint: won’t fix, because the booking-v3 form is retired. Ticket #2 closed as not planned.
  • one-day: the desk counts these reports under parser, so the parser fix closes them too. Ticket #3 closed as a duplicate of #1. Reopen it if one-day moves still come in after the fix.

  • parser: the shared parser reads every date month first, and these tenants write the day first. The report says a day and a month swapped, e.g. “1 March is now 3 January”. This is the fix already queued.
  • hint: the booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, but reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. People type what the hint tells them, and the date flips. Only reports sent through the form of a booking-v3 tenant. The parser fix does not touch it; the form hint (or the form’s reading) has to change.
  • one-day: the date moves by one day, not by a swapped month: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” It looks like a time zone or day-boundary bug. The desk already read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), but the cause table files them under parser by tenant.

How the reports were split

  1. One id, one report: the export lists some reports twice.
  2. The cause comes from the tenant (locale and booking form) and the channel, as causes.ts defines it, never from intakeTag. Intake guesses from the browser, and its guess disagrees with the cause on dozens of these reports.
  3. Reports the desk read as a one-day move are their own problem. The desk still counts them under their tenant’s cause, so the rows add up to more than the ticket holds.
  4. The subject line decides nothing. Many different subjects were merged into #4471, and one subject can belong to any of the three problems.
